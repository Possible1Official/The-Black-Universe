# Nysa survey excursion

After restoring all relays and initializing genesis, interact with **Scan the new world** on the bridge, then **Launch Kestrel shuttle**. The concourse shuttle-bay console also launches it. Pilot to Nysa's approach ring, brake below 18 m/s within 100 m and press E for guided touchdown. After orbital capture, clouds clear to reveal the actual survey terrain during a seven-second cockpit descent; you disembark after touchdown. A containment trip blocks departure; Balanced routing is recommended. See [flight controls and scope](FLIGHT.md). Orbital and local terrain representations remain separate; there is no seamless whole-planet streaming yet.

Nysa currently has one **256 × 256 meter** walkable survey sector. You disembark beside the visibly parked shuttle. Move, sprint, jump and look with the same desktop/touch controls used aboard. A circular landing pad marks the start; a luminous mineral outcrop is northeast at local X 9 / Z −9. Approach and face it, then press E (or the touch E button) to collect a sample. Return to the shuttle's aft hatch/beacon and board for the flight back. Return boarding/docking remains available before sampling and during a ship containment trip.

The rolling terrain is a deterministic **192 × 192 grid** with 1⅓-meter spacing: 37,249 vertices / 73,728 ground triangles. The exact indexed vertices feed both the Three.js mesh and a Rapier triangle collider. The landing region is flat, a broad raised ridge supports the weather station, rocks have sphere collisions, and the visible basalt perimeter matches the physical boundary. Instanced rocks/vegetation limit draw calls; rock placement leaves corridors between the pad, outcrop and field sites. Distant mountains and the moon are visual scenery. Vegetation does not collide; rock colliders approximate their geometry. This increases geometry/physics work and does not claim a hardware FPS gain or terrain streaming.

## Optional field exploration

Press **J** (including while the mouse is locked) or use the **FIELD LOG** button in the navigation card. It charts a basalt resonance arch at X −37 / Z −32 and a weather station on the eastern ridge at X 40 / Z −39. Choose **Track site** to replace surface compass guidance with that scanner. Walk to its illuminated console, face it and press E to record the reading. The scanner brightens after recording; the field log retains the text and completion count. **Restore mission guidance** returns to the outcrop or shuttle. Recording the tracked site also restores the mission waypoint. Neither scan is required to collect the sample, board or dock, and sites can be visited in either order. The arch pillars and station mast have matching box collisions; scanner sight cannot pass through a pillar. The dish is decorative and does not have its own collider.

The log remains readable aboard Erebus after genesis, with tracking disabled there. Its readings are authored observations (17.4 Hz resonance, 0.83 bar pressure and 6 m/s wind), not simulated weather or decoded alien intelligence. Scanning freezes walking through the existing dialogue; closing resumes exploration. This adds two persistent discoveries without inventory or an extra reward currency.

The ship and surface occupy separate physics coordinates; only the current environment is rendered. Crew patrols suspend during the excursion. Ship power continues while playing, including on the surface; pause freezes it. Transfer clears held input, horizontal/vertical velocity, nearest interaction and movement accumulation. The ship's previous object visibility is restored on extraction.

The additive `surveyed` flag and `fieldScans` array stay in the existing v2 save. Older v1/v2 saves default to false / empty scans; unknown, duplicate and malformed scan entries are filtered, and discoveries without completed genesis are rejected. Existing relay/power/world progression is retained. Location and tracked waypoint are deliberately transient: reload always starts aboard, retaining a collected sample and recorded scans. An older release ignores the new fields and may discard them on its next save, but can still read the mission and power data. The separate v1 rollback slot remains untouched. Reset on the surface returns aboard and clears all mission progress, including field scans.

Current limits: one bounded site, repeatable visits, one persistent sample, two optional scans and the [Silent Network expedition](RELAY-EXPEDITION.md) with an unlockable third landing pad; no inventory, weather simulation, global terrain streaming, fauna or surface NPCs. Bounded manual shuttle flight now connects the ship and site; see FLIGHT.md for its approximations. Runtime needs no credentials. Hardware GPU/mobile performance and real pointer lock require device testing.

`tests/survey.test.ts` verifies compatible sanitized saves, optional waypoint scope and real-Rapier travel from the pad through the outcrop and both scanners back to the pad, including interaction range and pillar occlusion. With the dev server running, `node scripts/survey-qa.mjs` checks rendered field-log tracking/scanning, short real walking approaches beyond the former boundary, dialogue suspension, quality changes, mobile layouts, docking, reload and reset. Walking poses are QA setup; flight uses actual controls.


## Ground materials

Nysa's ground uses smoothly blended sand ripples, fractured stone and organic soil patterns. The existing regional colors distinguish dunes, basalt, wetlands and highlands; slopes expose stone, while highlands mix stone and sand. One deterministic seamless128×128 linear RGB texture packs the three scalar fields. A second rotated, larger-scale sample modulates the color to reduce obvious four-meter repetition. Local X/Z coordinates keep texture density fixed as the character/camera moves; there is no animated noise.

High mode derives bump detail from the blended fine height field and gives organic soil lower roughness than dry stone/sand. Performance retains both color samples and uniform matte roughness, disabling bump/roughness detail. Quality changes reuse one material/map; contact occlusion still uses the same receiver. These are generated shading patterns, not scanned ground textures or new physical displacement. Existing terrain/collider vertices, vegetation, water and CGTrader props remain unchanged.

The shared RGBA map holds65,536 B (64 KiB), down from192 KiB across three earlier maps; a full mip chain is about85 KiB before driver overhead. A new three-component blend attribute adds446,988 B (~437 KiB) to the37,249 terrain vertices in CPU memory and the uploaded GPU buffer. Net allocation therefore increases despite the smaller texture. Both quality modes now take two packed color samples (previously Performance one); High reuses their fine scalar value for screen derivatives/roughness rather than sampling separate normal/roughness maps. Extra interpolation and arithmetic add GPU work. No additional geometry, draw, download, light, collision query, shadow pass or depth-writing floor overlay. No physical-device frame/memory measurement or FPS improvement is claimed; this is still a bounded procedural landscape.

With the dev server running, `node scripts/surface-qa.mjs .local/qa/surface.json .local/qa/surface-screens` records matched 960×600 landing/outcrop views through performance→High→performance. Create the screenshot directory first. The probe seeds its own isolated completed save, deploys through the actual science controls, uses dev pose hooks for viewpoints and checks material/ship-light policies. High draw counters cover the final compositor pass, not total scene draws.

## Field map and survey waypoints

Open FIELD LOG/J after creating Nysa to inspect its north-up 256-meter sector chart. On the surface, the white arrow marks your current position/heading; labeled markers show Kestrel, sample, two scan sites and coolant supplies. Tap any map position or enter local X/Z coordinates to track a temporary survey waypoint. Pins stay three meters inside the sector perimeter. INFO exposes the existing heading/distance guidance; it is a straight line, so route around physical obstacles yourself. Getting within two meters restores mission guidance. FIELD LOG → Restore mission guidance cancels a pin; travel, reset and reload also clear it. Pins never complete samples/scans or alter mission saves. Aboard Erebus the same chart is read-only and displays retained discoveries. Close field log is reachable while scrolling; the chart stays closed during normal play. This is a fully charted local sector rather than fog-of-war or a global planetary map.

### Wetland vegetation

The old floating cone grass is replaced by64 grass clumps from Nicholas-3D's reviewed free CGTrader pack. Two small textured variants share instanced geometry and cutout/normal maps. Sites follow the actual terrain mesh and avoid standing-water centers, steep slopes, rocks and mission props. Clump bases embed slightly; individual leaf roots do not deform to every terrain point. They are decorative and nonblocking. A small root-anchored sway freezes with pause/dialogue and while away from Nysa; it does not react to vehicles, collision or real wind forces. The source contains no animation clips. Leaf diffuse lighting approximates its Blender translucency without making leaves emit light.

Loading starts on surface entry. A failed download retains simple rooted blades and retries on explicit re-entry; successful geometry/maps are reused.64 clumps in two draws add160 triangles over the old240 cones,648 KB of cold transfer and about4 MiB decoded map pixels (~5.33 MiB with mipmaps, before driver overhead). Alpha cutouts/normal sampling, shader motion and diffuse wrapping add GPU work; real-device budgets are unmeasured. See ASSETS.md for source/license/conversion evidence.

### Pond and shoreline

The wetland pond fills the connected depression around local X32/Z34 at the existing water level, clipped to the same terrain triangles used for rendering and physics. It no longer ends at an arbitrary15-meter circle while the ground is still submerged. Separate depressions stay dry; the water level and terrain/vehicle collision do not change. Shallows fade into the bank; depth changes color/opacity, with subtle moving surface normals and an angle-dependent constant sky-color reflection approximation. High adds a second ripple family. Ripple time freezes during pause/dialogue and away from the visible surface; quality changes/re-entry reuse geometry/material.

The surface stays flat: ripples affect shading only. It does not simulate flow, refraction, scene reflections, swimming, underwater fog or buoyancy, and has no collision layer. Existing terrain movement through the shallow basin remains. One transparent water draw remains, now1,052 triangles instead of48 and88,368 B of vertex attributes instead of1,888 B, plus one-time terrain connectivity/clipping work. Fragment depth/opacity/Fresnel math and one/two ripple cosine terms add GPU work; High can receive existing light shadows. No downloaded texture, new light, reflection render target/pass or shadow caster. Real-device FPS/memory remain unmeasured.


## Silent Network and Relay pad

The [Silent Network guide](RELAY-EXPEDITION.md) covers two new receiver repairs and the walk-in outpost. FIELD LOG can track the expedition alongside independent scans and supplies. Activating the outpost after both repairs unlocks Relay pad at X76/Z−55 in PAD/L. All three pads have prepared flat ground and rock-free vehicle/hatch clearances; entering a new pad relocates the parked shuttle/vehicles and extraction waypoint consistently. The outpost and third pad occupy the existing sector rather than a second planet.
