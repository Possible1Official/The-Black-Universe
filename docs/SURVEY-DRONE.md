# ECHO survey companion

Complete Silent Network (repair both receivers, activate the communications uplink), then find ECHO beside the outpost. J / FIELD LOG → Locate ECHO provides direction and distance. Approach and press E to recover it; choose Follow me, Hold here or Scan nearby survey site. The command panel closes after a choice and adds no permanent screen clutter.

ECHO follows your walked route on foot, stopping about2.6m behind along that route. If a rock or corner blocks its swept volume, it tries a short collision-checked detour, then rejoins the saved route. It can adjust height over supported shallow slopes; sealed or tight routes still make it hold. Four source-animated propellers turn while its local stabilization holds altitude, including before pairing. Movement and animation freeze while paused, in dialogue or away from Nysa. Driving a bike/rover makes it hold; it does not chase moving vehicles. The field log can locate it again. Completing recovery persists locally; on each new landing the recovered unit deploys beside the selected Kestrel pad in Hold mode. Reload starts aboard, retaining recovery and completed readings. Reset clears them.

Scan records an existing unread arch/weather reading within8m and clear sight after two active seconds. It holds while scanning; choose Follow again afterward. Range and occlusion are checked again before awarding a reading. It cannot scan through walls, reveal distant sites or replace the sample objective.

## Bounds and verification

This is a bounded scripted companion, not a general autonomous agent. A maximum256-point trail records walked corners; a swept56cm-radius sphere prevents passing through solid geometry. It adds no collider that could block walking. After0.2 active seconds of blockage, local A* searches a9×9 grid with1.2m spacing (4.8m in each horizontal axis), only for a next breadcrumb within6m. Up to80 supporting-floor rays and160 candidate sphere sweeps are allowed per search; edges change height by at most1.1m. Every executed step is swept again, so moving obstacles cannot invalidate collision safety. A successful path replaces only the blocked trail segment. Search cooldown is2.5 active seconds; an unsuccessful obstruction still stops following after2 seconds, avoiding repeated failed searches. Tight/low spaces, long walls, unsupported ground, query-budget exhaustion and abrupt separation can therefore require a new Follow command from a clear position. It never warps through a wall. Hold, Scan, boarding and redeployment discard pending detour points; pause freezes them. Terrain following remains kinematic, without rotor aerodynamics or global pathfinding.

The derivative costs97,083 encoded bytes /224,360 decoded GLB bytes,7,302 unique geometry triangles and15,366 rendered triangles over8 mesh instances, with four shared rotor meshes. It loads only on entering Nysa; failed imports retain a simple geometric fallback and retry on explicit re-entry. It adds no image maps, lights, shadow maps or collision handles. Unblocked following uses one ground ray plus one sphere sweep per active fixed step (up to60Hz); obstruction searches add the bounded CPU/query work described above, without changing rendering. Held units avoid those queries. Shadows can participate in the existing High-mode shadow pass. Physical-device FPS/memory and hosted rendering remain unmeasured.

Meaningful tests cover right-angle routes, bounded history, held/blocked/separated behavior, real Rapier wall sweeps, actual packaged model/materials/rotor motion and bounds, retry behavior, pause, scan timing/visibility, and save compatibility. Mobile-emulated browser evidence and limitations are recorded in PROGRESS. Distant-site fixtures skip unchanged travel; they do not demonstrate a full expedition drive.
