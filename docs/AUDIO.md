# Movement and vehicle audio

The existing menu checkbox now controls **Game audio**: ambience, player footsteps, landings, mounted engines/rolling and interaction cues. Its enabled/muted preference saves locally, independently of mission resets. Audio starts only from Play/Resume or an enabled in-game user gesture; a muted start creates no AudioContext. Unsupported audio fails silently without affecting play.

Inside Erebus, two low sine tones provide machinery ambience, quieter during a containment trip. Nysa instead has filtered wind. Orbital flight has no outdoor wind or ship-room hum: Kestrel's sound represents interior motor/vibration feedback. These are locally synthesized effects, not recordings, spatial acoustics or physically simulated sound propagation. Surface wind is not occluded inside small buildings.

On foot, distance actually moved by the character controller advances cadence, using the actual Cyber Biker captured walking/running strides1.465755/3.50991m, two steps per cycle. Audio and visuals share these constants; the Ely fallback retains its1.313094/2.2m values. Running has longer strides, not necessarily more steps per second at the configured speeds. This matches distance cadence, not individual imported heel-contact keyframes. Holding movement against a wall produces no steps. Airborne movement stays silent until a sufficiently long descent ends on supported ground; initial spawn settling, discontinuous placement, pause and reset do not queue a landing burst. Landing strength follows bounded downward speed. A supporting-floor ray only when a step/landing occurs distinguishes imported metal decks/props and rock proxies from the shared terrain; dune, basalt/highland and wetland regions select sand, stone and damp-soil filters.

Mounted Turbo Bike has a higher motor tone, rover a lower one, and Kestrel a separate midrange tone. Pitch responds to actual normalized speed plus requested motor load. Surface rolling noise follows actual speed, including while braking. Brake suppresses requested thrust/boost sound immediately while retaining idle motor/rolling; visual propulsion and gameplay forces are unchanged. Runway departure/return and guided flight use existing guidance state for authored motor feedback. These effects do not add gearboxes, torque or tire/friction physics.

Pause/tab hiding mutes the shared output, cancels active one-shots and suspends AudioContext processing. Resume reuses the same graph after a gesture. Muting also suspends it; enabling while paused waits for Resume. Dialogue clears foot cadence and leaves ambience available for interaction cues. All cues pass through the same mute/limiter path.

## Bounds and evidence

One graph has16 persistent nodes/six continuously running sources while enabled, with one shared two-second mono Float32 noise buffer (384,000 bytes at48kHz, excluding browser overhead). At most eight temporary voices coexist; steps/landings use four nodes each, cues two, disconnecting on completion. Steps closer than80ms are coalesced instead of creating catch-up bursts. The mix uses80ms parameter smoothing and one output compressor. It adds no sample downloads, model/image resources, graphics draws/lights or physics colliders. At most one extra short ground ray per emitted movement event; no per-frame audio ray/occlusion search. Device CPU, output latency, loudness and speaker/headphone quality remain unmeasured.

`tests/soundscape.test.ts` checks distance/frame partition cadence, mute/placement/air suppression, actual Rapier wall/jump movement and scene/engine/brake selection. `scripts/movement-audio-qa.mjs` renders actual OfflineAudioContext waveforms to check output, material spectra, motor pitch, brake attenuation, silence, voice limits and cleanup, then uses real game controls for walking/jumping, driving/braking and menu lifecycle. Temporary scene/vehicle/flight setup skips unchanged missions and travel. This is signal/browser verification, not human listening or physical-device speaker validation.
