# Kestrel shuttle flight

Restore the relays and create Nysa first. The bridge science array's **Launch Kestrel shuttle** button boards the vehicle; the physical **Launch survey shuttle** console at X 5 / Z 15 in the habitat concourse provides the same launch. A ship containment trip blocks outbound departure. Kestrel exits Erebus's exterior shuttle bay over three simulation seconds, then hands you manual control.

The cockpit view contains a cyan approach ring and flight telemetry. W raises throttle; S lowers it. A/D or Left/Right turn. Up/Down pitch, and mouse look or right-side touch drag also aims the craft. Shift boosts from the normal 110 m/s target speed to 180 m/s. Space or the held BRAKE button cuts throttle and slows the craft. On touch, push the existing joystick up/down for throttle and left/right to turn; hold the BOOST/BRAKE buttons as needed. T or the TARGET button switches between Nysa and Erebus during manual flight, permitting an aborted trip or a repeat visit. This changes guidance, not position or heading.

Fly toward the marked ring. Within 100 sector meters and below 18 m/s, press E (or touch E) to initiate landing/docking. Remote and fast approaches are rejected. The final three seconds use guided motion; landing then loads the existing Nysa survey sector and places you beside your parked shuttle. Walk to the luminous outcrop and collect the sample. Return to the aft hatch/beacon and interact with **Board shuttle for Erebus**. Kestrel lifts off, you pilot back, then brake at Erebus's ring and dock with E to return to the habitat concourse.

Erebus, Nysa and the universe forge have exterior representations visible in the bounded flight scene. Nysa has generated globe-space ocean/land/coast/ice colors and a separate drifting transparent cloud map. One local survey site is accessible; the orbital globe and walking terrain are separate representations. The small sector uses compressed distances and scale, not real planetary dimensions. This is an arcade flight foundation with assisted inertial velocity, not Newtonian orbital mechanics, atmospheric aerodynamics or a seamless whole-planet descent.

Movement runs through the same bounded fixed 60 Hz clock as walking. Broad spherical hulls around Nysa/Erebus prevent penetration and cut velocity/throttle on contact; they approximate visible geometry. Crossing the 2,300-meter sector boundary safely recovers at the current approach ring. No hull damage, fuel cost or death is added. The forge and stars are scenery without detailed collision. Walking on Nysa still uses Rapier terrain/prop collisions, including an approximate central shuttle hull; decorative wings are not separately colliding.

Pause freezes flight state, and input cleanup releases touch braking/boost and the joystick. Crew and walking physics suspend during flight. Ship power continues while playing; a later trip does not prevent landing or returning/docking. Flight/location remains transient: reload starts aboard and retains valid mission/sample progression. Reset in flight returns aboard and clears the mission. No new save version, account, key, external asset or flight library is required.

Tests: `tests/flight.test.ts` covers takeoff, speed guards, actual piloted round-trip travel, boost/braking, collision/boundary recovery, steering and equivalent fixed-step partitions. `tests/planet.test.ts` covers longitude continuity and ocean/land/ice colors. `node scripts/flight-qa.mjs` pilots an isolated completed mission with actual controls, samples Nysa, docks, verifies pause/remote-landing rejection, real touch thrust and reset/reload. Dev setup hooks position only the walking visitor; no shuttle-pose hook is used. Broader mission/surface fixtures use the same real-control flight helper.

Next: longer approach corridors, richer exterior craft/hangar detail, visible atmospheric descent, larger streamed landing zones and more grounded NPC/ship systems. The long-term target is the freedom and immersion of Infinity; this release implements one bounded flight/exploration loop and does not claim that level of realism or agency.
