# Kestrel shuttle flight

Restore the relays and create Nysa first. Kestrel is always parked near the entrance of the bow runway while aboard. Use the inner-door panel, walk through the doorway and board at the cyan starboard marker with E. Boarding closes the inner door, then opens the outer space door; the jet waits for both conditions before a five-second guided runway departure and manual flight. A containment trip blocks outbound boarding. The existing science-array launch button and concourse launch console remain shortcuts that seat you in the same parked jet and run the same interlock. The bedroom is now directly aft of the lounge, clear of the space exit.

The cockpit view contains a cyan approach ring and flight telemetry. W raises throttle; S lowers it. A/D or Left/Right turn. Up/Down pitch, and mouse look or right-side touch drag also aims the craft. Shift boosts from the normal 110 m/s target speed to 180 m/s. Space or the held BRAKE button cuts throttle and slows the craft. On touch, push the existing joystick up/down for throttle and left/right to turn; hold the BOOST/BRAKE buttons as needed. T or the TARGET button switches between Nysa and Erebus during manual flight, permitting an aborted trip or a repeat visit. This changes guidance, not position or heading.

Fly toward the marked ring. Within 100 sector meters and below 18 m/s, press E (or touch E) to initiate landing/docking. Remote and fast approaches are rejected. Nysa's three-second orbital capture now leads through a cloud transition into a seven-second cockpit descent over the actual survey terrain. Radar altitude falls from 46 m to touchdown, with the illuminated landing pad below. You then disembark beside your parked shuttle. Walk to the luminous outcrop and collect the sample. Return to the aft hatch/beacon and interact with **Board shuttle for Erebus**. A seven-second cockpit ascent shows the terrain receding, then a cloud transition and three-second orbital departure return manual controls. Pilot back, brake at Erebus's ring and dock with E to return through the bow runway.

Erebus, Nysa and the universe forge have exterior representations visible in the bounded flight scene. Nysa has generated globe-space ocean/land/coast/ice colors and a separate drifting transparent cloud map. One local survey site is accessible; the orbital globe and walking terrain remain separate representations, concealed at their handoff by a brief procedural cloud cover. The surface descent/ascent actually renders the walking terrain with the cockpit and is guided rather than freely piloted. The small sector uses compressed distances and scale, not real planetary dimensions. This is an arcade flight foundation with assisted inertial velocity, not Newtonian orbital mechanics, atmospheric aerodynamics or seamless whole-planet streaming.

Movement runs through the same bounded fixed 60 Hz clock as walking. Nysa retains a spherical safety hull; Erebus now uses the same seven exterior box dimensions for rendering and manual-flight collision. A conservative 3.5-meter axis clearance around those boxes approximates the craft; contact cuts velocity/throttle and recovers to a nearby point outside the entire hull union, with 0.01 m extra clearance against rounding repeats. The open hangar is navigable instead of blocked by the former station sphere. This is an approximate axis-aligned envelope, not detailed hull/wing contact or swept rigid-body physics. Lights and trim are decorative. Crossing the 2,300-meter sector boundary safely recovers at the current approach ring. No hull damage, fuel cost or death is added. The forge and stars are scenery without detailed collision. Walking on Nysa still uses Rapier terrain/prop collisions, including an approximate central shuttle hull; decorative wings are not separately colliding.

Pause freezes flight state, and input cleanup releases touch braking/boost and the joystick. Crew and walking physics suspend during flight. Ship power continues while playing; a later trip does not prevent landing or returning/docking. Flight/location remains transient: reload starts aboard and retains valid mission/sample progression. Reset in flight returns aboard and clears the mission. No new save version, account, key, external asset or flight library is required.

## Approach instruments

Three reusable approach beacons sit at the destination and 50/100 meters along its outbound corridor. They are cyan in cruise, amber while **BRAKE NOW** is active, and green when approach requirements permit E landing/docking. The beacons guide you; Nysa retains its speed/range landing gate and Erebus additionally requires its front approach or clear open bay. The amber **VELOCITY** diamond projects actual motion direction into the forward cockpit view, so during a turn it can differ from the central aim crosshair. It hides when stopped, outside the forward display, paused, on foot or in guided flight.

The navigation hint reports signed closing speed, lateral drift, and estimated distance/time to stop. The forecast shares the actual assisted-thrust/braking update, uses fixed 60 Hz steps, ends below 0.5 m/s and caps work at 300 steps (five simulation seconds). It holds your current heading and brake; it ignores future steering, hull/sector recovery and obstacles. It is an unobstructed-flight estimate rather than collision avoidance or Newtonian navigation. **BRAKE NOW** triggers while approaching faster than the allowed landing speed when projected stopping travel plus a 15-meter margin reaches the 100-meter capture zone. It can conservatively stop you outside the zone: use gentle thrust to close the remaining gap and brake again. **TARGET RECEDING** warns when velocity is carrying you away from the selected goal.

Space / held BRAKE now takes priority over simultaneous W, forward joystick and BOOST. Braking cuts throttle at 1.8 per second, suppresses boost and damps velocity at the existing braking rate, even when other controls remain held. Releasing the brake restores normal thrust/boost intent. No autopilot, additional destination, fuel/damage system or new save field is introduced.

`tests/approach.test.ts` compares forecasts to actual stopped flight with lateral/vertical drift, verifies held-control brake priority, closing/receding/readiness cues and shared beacon geometry/material isolation. `node scripts/approach-qa.mjs` checks actual steering/velocity-marker drift, target switching, early warning, keyboard thrust+boost+brake, pause, touchdown/return, preserved field scans and real three-touch joystick+BOOST+BRAKE followed by reset. Production QA verifies the warning, velocity display and ready state before the existing descent/field-log flow.

## Guided capture clearance

Nysa's three-second orbital capture now follows a normalized radial arc, preserving at least the existing eight-meter planet safety margin; the previous straight chord could cut that margin for an offset approach. Orbital and local walking representations remain separate, with the same cloud handoff and seven-second surface descent/ascent. During orbital capture the cockpit smoothly aligns toward Nysa rather than retaining an unrelated manual heading.

Erebus docking still needs ≤100 m range and ≤18 m/s, plus a clear front approach (Z ≤ −36) or the open bay (|X| ≤19.5, |Y| ≤1.5, Z −36…46 outside the hull). Nearby side, rear or roof shortcuts display **FRONT APPROACH REQUIRED**. From the front, a five-second guided path spends three seconds centering at Z −40 before a two-second ingress to Z −20. From inside the clear bay it moves directly to that endpoint over five seconds. The cockpit eases toward the bay interior, and three generated floor strips help identify it. You can manually enter the open bay before requesting docking; E remains necessary to disembark aboard. This is guided capture, not an autonomous general pathfinder.

`tests/capture.test.ts` reproduces the former chord-clearance issue and samples legal capture arcs, checks docking paths against all rendered box dimensions plus clearance, rejects side/rear requests, pilots actual fixed-step entry, checks intersecting-hull recovery and verifies rendered dimensions and view direction. `node scripts/capture-qa.mjs` uses actual controls for manual bay entry, interior docking, outbound capture/view/pause/quality changes, surface descent/ascent, staged return ingress and retained scans/reload. Walking fixture setup only; no flight pose hook.

Next: richer exterior craft detail and an additional landing site connected through flight. The long-term target is the freedom and immersion of Infinity; this release implements one bounded flight/exploration loop and does not claim that level of realism or agency.
